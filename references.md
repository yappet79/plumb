# Layout archetypes

The module's visual literacy. Not pictures and not copies — **a reading of how each one is
built**: what holds it up, when it fits, what it costs. Copying someone's layout is wrong
legally and pointless practically; what transfers is structure, scale and discipline.

Studied live in the Framer gallery, 02 Aug 2026, plus a reading of our own strong work.
**Not studied:** Mobbin and Screenlane (now pageflows) — both behind a paid subscription.

## Why this is separate from the brief

Measured the same day: quality was lifted by **measurable floors** (nothing below 12px, card
padding no less than 20px, white ground, one device for every number) — three engines out of
three complied. The request to "make it beautiful, your way" produced 9px type and cream paper.

Hence a distinction that is easy to blur:
**constraints raise the floor · archetypes widen the spread.** Both are needed.
Without archetypes sol/terra/luna diverge at random; with them, on purpose.

## Landing pages and promo

**Poster typography.** A giant display face filling the screen, photography incidental or
absent, one loud colour on a neutral. Held up by size and by how the letters are cropped.
Fits where the name matters more than the description: portfolio, studio, event.
*Costs:* very little text fits; there is nowhere to explain a product.

**Editorial spread.** Display headline left, a portrait or object right, a narrow column of
manifesto in small grotesque between them. Held up by the contrast of sizes (headline to body
around 6:1) and a strict grid.
*Costs:* demands strong photography; falls apart on stock.

**Mockup showcase.** The product's screen in a device frame on a coloured ground, headline
above, button below. The most common and the safest. Held up by actually showing the product.
*Costs:* looks like everyone else; creates no distinction.

**Sticker collage.** Colour panels, handwritten marks, stickers, deliberate unseriousness.
Held up by density and the rhythm of small elements.
*Costs:* dates instantly and undermines trust wherever a client's money is involved.

**Dark showcase.** Near-black ground, one cold accent, a large photograph or 3D object,
centred headline. Held up by one object glowing in the dark.
*Costs:* collapses on a projector and in print — the midtones die.

**Catalogue grid.** A grid of equal-weight cards, each a work or a service. Held up by the
uniformity of the card and by exactly one deviation from it (one card larger).
*Costs:* without that deviation it reads as a table.

## Product interfaces

**The evidence rail** (our case, `triage2-sol`). Grouped navigation on the left, the work in
the centre, a narrow "why" column on the right: quotes, confidence, source. Held up by the
machine showing the basis for a decision next to the decision.
*Fits:* anything where a human checks after an agent. *Costs:* the third column eats width.

**Master–detail with a queue.** A list of tasks above or beside, the selected one expanded
below. Held up by showing both the flow and the single case without navigating away.
*Costs:* on a narrow screen the detail slides down and gets lost.

**Instrument panel with one repeating device** (an evaluation console). Rings or bars of a
single kind for every number, and underneath **a conclusion in plain language**: "two thirds
of the misses come from retrieval/chunking, owner: retrieval".
*Held up by:* the repetition, and by numbers that end in a conclusion rather than in themselves.
*Costs:* the device must hold everywhere; one exception breaks the system.

**Dense operational table.** Rows of 40px or more, columns with explicit labels, state as a
coloured dot rather than a filled row. Held up by row rhythm and colour restraint.
*Costs:* dull unless one accent element carries it.

**Canvas and inspector.** The work area fills the screen, properties live in a sliding panel.
Held up by the object of work always being larger than the interface around it.
*Costs:* the inspector becomes a junk drawer unless it is cut into sections.

## What transfers every time

- **Contrast of sizes**, not the count of them: three sizes stepping by 1.5–1.6 read better
  than seven stepping by 1.1.
- **One accent** for actions and selection. Green and red only as direction of change, never
  as decoration.
- **One repeating device** for every number on the screen. A different treatment per block is
  an assembly of parts, not a system.
- **A conclusion in words.** A screen that ends in figures hands the work back to the reader.
- **Structural devices encode information.** Numbering only if the order is real; a meter only
  if there is a measurement behind it.
